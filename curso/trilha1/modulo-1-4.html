MODULE 1.4 · WALKTHROUGH · ~45 min

🎬 From the 78-minute meeting to the minutes that everyone reads

Let's follow Patrícia, operations manager, from Wednesday 3:35 pm (she left a long meeting with a tense WhatsApp from the CEO) until Thursday 9:12 am (CEO replied "perfect, thank you for the clarity"). You will see every prompt, every output, every error, every adjustment. No cutting.

How to read this module:don't skip the "failed outputs". The value of the walkthrough is precisely to see where things almost went wrong — and the exact adjustment that unlocked it. This is what the prompt engineering book never shows.

============ SETUP ============
⚙

Setup — Wednesday, 3:35 pm

Who:Patrícia, 33, operations manager at a medical technology company with 90 employees. 4 years at home. It's not an "early adopter" — it uses Claude sometimes, at the basic level of the chat.

What happened:You have just left a meeting with 6 people about the approval pipeline for a new device at Anvisa. Meeting lasted 78 minutes. It was recorded on Zoom (Patricia has access to automatic transcription, ~14 thousand words of raw text).

Pressure:3:33 pm WhatsApp arrived from the CEO:

"Pat, you were on the call just now, right? Give me the summary. I need to know by when the approval will be ready, how many blockers we have and who owns each one. Council tomorrow at 9am."

Stakes:Council is 9am. CEO needs to present with confidence. Patricia has (ideally) until the end of the day. He got tired of taking notes — he forgot half of it. Her next call is 5pm, so there is ~1h20.

============ ITERATION 0 ============
0

Attempt 0 — the bad reflex (15:38)

Patrícia opens Claude on her cell phone (still in the car), copies the entire Zoom transcript, and types:

PATRICIA →
Give me a summary of this meeting

[pastes 14k words]

Claude returns, 8 seconds later:

← CLAUDE
The meeting discussed the approval process of a new medical device with Anvisa. Various technical, regulatory and operational aspects were discussed. The team aligned expectations about deadlines and responsibilities, identified pending challenges and defined next steps. Participants demonstrated commitment to the project and agreed to resume the discussion next week...

😬 What happened

Patrícia read it, felt butterflies in her stomach:this is of no use. It has no date. It has no name. There is no concrete blocker. Does not answer the CEO's question. If she sends that, the CEO responds "Pat, call me."

Why it failed (and why it happens to EVERYONE in N1):

  • • She asked for whatseemedobvious ("meeting summary") rather than whatreally needed(answer to the CEO's 3 questions)
  • • The critical context was not passed: the CEO's question, who are the players, who is the final audience (Council)
  • • Did not ask for format
  • • Didn't say what was important to extract (dates, owners, blockers) versus what was noise (small talk, repetitions, technical digressions)
============ ITERATION 1 ============
1

Iteration 1 — first adjustment (15:42)

Patrícia takes a breath, thinks "ok, what does the CEO NEED?". Rewrite:

PATRICIA →
Act as senior executive secretary. I will paste the transcript of a 78-minute meeting about Anvisa approval. My CEO asked for the summary now. He needs to know: 1. Until when the approval is ready 2. How many blockers do we have 3. Who owns each one His presentation to the Council is tomorrow at 9am. Give me this summary focused on these 3 questions. Bullet format. [pastes 14k words]

Claude returns:

← CLAUDE
**Approval deadline:** The team is working with an estimated deadline of between the end of June and mid-July 2026, subject to the completion of efficacy tests. **Identified blockers:** - Delay in clinical efficacy report - Pending technical documentation from the packaging supplier - Awaiting return from Anvisa on the partial dossier submitted in February - Need to review IFU due to feedback from the regulatory consultant **Responsible persons mentioned:** Several team members were cited, including professionals from the technical, regulatory and commercial areas. Specific responsibilities were discussed during the meeting.

🟡 Better — but still no good

Patrícia reads and realizes:better, but it’s still not possible to send it to the CEO. Problems:

  • • Deadline is vague ("end of June and mid-July") when in the meeting someone saida specific date
  • • Blockers are ownerless — exactly the CEO's question ("who owns each one")
  • • "Miscellaneous members" is the most useless phrase in an executive summary
  • • Claude invented "regulatory consultant" — Patrícia doesn't remember this being mentioned. Claude may have hallucinated
============ ITERATION 2 ============
2

Iteration 2 — tightening context and veto (15:48)

Patricia returns. Now she knows what's missing. Rewrites with 3 important changes:

PATRICIA →
Same meeting. I will re-order with stricter rules. Act as a senior executive secretary working in medical technology company. You know the jargon (Anvisa, IFU, dossier, RDC). You know CEO hates vagueness. Critical context: - 78-minute meeting on approval of new device at Anvisa - 6 people present (I will list them at the end) - Final summary hearing: CEO, who will present to the Board tomorrow at 9am - CEO hates: vague names, dates in "ranges" without premises, blocker without owner Task: generate report minutes with EXACTLY this structure: ## Direct response to the CEO (3 bullets) - Approval deadline: [specific DATE + premise that justifies] - Total active blockers: [number] - Summary in 1 sentence ## Schedule | Landmark | Date | Dependency | [3-5 lines] ## Blockers (1 table) | # | Blocker | Owner (name) | Expected resolution date | Impact if not resolved | [1 line per blocker] ## Decisions made at the meeting (not recommendations, decisions) - ... ## What was NOT decided (goes to the Council) - ... ## Participants - Name — role (decision you made) RULES: - If the transcription does not have a specific owner's name, write "TBD — speak to [person left to collect]". DON'T MAKE UP A NAME. - If the transcript does not have a specific date, write "It was not said at the meeting" and that's it. - DO NOT use "several", "some", "several", "discussed". Each line needs to have an actor and action. - Don't invent facts. If you're not sure, mark it with [?] and I'll validate it. Transcription: [pastes 14k words]

Claude returns (cropped):

← CLAUDE
## Direct response to the CEO - **Approval deadline:** August 12, 2026 (premise: effectiveness report delivered by Jun 30 and final dossier submitted by Jul 15) - **Total active blockers:** 3 (1 critical, 2 important) - In 1 sentence: the critical path goes through the effectiveness report — 18 days late and follows the blocker that defines the rest of the schedule. ## Schedule | Landmark | Date | Dependency | |---|---|---| | Delivery of efficacy report (CRO Bioteste) | 30/jun/2026 | Data collection from 12 patients completed — 8 already done | | Final consolidated dossier | 15/Jul/2026 | Efficacy report + IFU review | | Anvisa Submission | 20/Jul/2026 | Final dossier + ART from the technical manager | | Approval estimate | 12/Aug/2026 | Premise: Anvisa analysis in standard flow (~20 days) | ##Blockers | # | Blocker | Owner | Resolution | Impact if not resolved | |---|---|---|---|---| | 1 | 18-day delay in the effectiveness report (CRO delayed recruitment) | Marcelo (RT) — in contact with Bioteste | 30/jun | The Council window bursts. No report, no dossier. | | 2 | Technical documentation from packaging supplier (NimTec) pending | TBA — Patrícia checked with purchases | 25/jun | Delays dossier by ~5 days. There is supplier B already evaluated, it is a plan B. | | 3 | IFU Review Pending Internal Regulatory Feedback | Camila (Regulatory) | 28/jun | You can reopen the final version of the dossier if you require changes. | ## Decisions made at the meeting - Maintain Bioteste as CRO (Cromos alternative would cost 22 days more) - Plan B with packaging supplier activated if NimTec does not deliver by Jun 22 - Camila has the autonomy to approve IFU without a new committee meeting ## What was NOT decided - If we are going to communicate the delay to the Board BEFORE the Thursday meeting (CEO decides) - If we hire an external regulatory consultant to review the final dossier before submission [?] ## Participants - Marcelo Veiga — RT (technical manager, owner of blocker #1) - Camila Santos — Regulatory (owner of blocker #3) - Patrícia Lima — Operations (I will raise NimTec status) - Roberto Alves — Commercial (did not make a decision) - Daniel Oliveira — Finance (validated extra budget of R$ 14k for plan B packaging) - Lucia Pereira — Clinical support (did not make decision) [?] flag: in the transcript the suggestion of "external regulatory consultant" quickly appeared but no decision was made. I recommend you validate with Marcelo.

✅ Now yes

Patrícia reads it and feels relief.This has what the CEO needs.Specific date, blockers with owner, schedule with premise. The [?] flag is brilliant — Claude marked where he had doubts instead of inventing.

The 3 changes that unlocked everything:

  1. 1. Exact output structurewith headers and tables — Claude stops "narrating" and starts "filling slots".
  2. 2. Anti-vague rules("don't use 'miscellaneous', 'several'; each line must have an actor and action") — forces specificity.
  3. 3. Permission to say "I don't know"([?] / "It wasn't said at the meeting") — kills hallucination. This is perhaps the most underrated ingredient in N1.

Iteration 3 — CEO asks for another cut (4:32 p.m.)

Patricia sends. CEO responds in 4 minutes:

"Excellent. Now give me the version that I will PASTE on the Board's slide. Maximum 6 lines. Board language, not operations language. And a risk phrase at the end, without alarmism."

Patrícia returns to Claude —same conversation, so the context is already there:

PATRICIA →
CEO now asked: Board slide version. Maximum 6 lines. Advice Language (non-operational). Risk phrase at the end, without alarmism. Use the content you already have from the meeting. No need to repeat transcription.
← CLAUDE
**Status — Anvisa approval** - Submission scheduled for **July 20th**; approval expected on **August 12, 2026**. - Three fronts in monitoring: efficacy report, packaging documentation and IFU review. - The critical front is the **effectiveness report**, with a recoverable delay of 18 days; Contracted CRO committed to delivery on June 30th. - Alternative packaging supplier plan is validated and can be activated if necessary on June 22nd. - Current budget includes contingency (additional R$ 14k already approved internally). **Risk to monitor:** further delay in the efficacy report compresses the submission window and could push approval to the end of August.

Patrícia copies, pastes on the slide, sends it to the CEO 4:38 pm.CEO responds "perfect". Patrícia returns to her 5pm call with 22 minutes left.

============ DEPLOY / RESULT ============
✓

Thursday, 9:12 am — feedback

CEO finished the presentation to the Council. He sent it to Patrícia:

"Pat, perfect, thank you for the clarity. Council approved the plan. Only request: create the same structure as a fixed template for all critical meetings from now on. Share it with the people."

Patrícia, in the shower in the morning, realized something: the gain was not “Claude quickly summarized”. It was that shegot two DIFFERENT exits from the same meeting(one operational, one Council) without rereading 14 thousand words twice. This is the real unlocking.

============ LESSONS ============
★

Lessons from the journey

1. "Summary" is the wrong output 90% of the time

What seems like an obvious request ("that sums it up for me") is almost never what you need. Right question:"What does the PERSON WHO IS READING NEED TO KNOW?". Iteration 0 asked for summary. Iteration 2 asked for "answer to CEO's 3 questions + tabulated structure". Different output, totally different value.

2. Exact structure > abstract instructions

"Make a good summary" doesn't work. "Header ## You design the output, Claude executes it.

3. Explicit permission to say "I don't know" kills hallucinations

It was the most underrated ingredient of iteration 2. "Don't make it up. If you're not sure, mark it with [?]." — this explicit command prevents Claude from fabricating names, dates, quotes. Without it, he will invent things to "be useful".

4. Continue the conversation > start from scratch

Iteration 3 was quick because Patrícia used the SAME conversation. Context was already there. If she had opened a new chat, she would have had to retrieve the transcript and re-explain it. Continuity within a session is "L1 being done well" — there is no need to move up to L2 (Projects) yet.

5. The real gain is RE-USABILITY

CEO asked "make this become a template". Then Patrícia saved the iteration 2 prompt in a doc. Every critical meeting from now on, 30 seconds to adapt. When you feel this, you are ready to move up to N2 (turn into a fixed project).

============ ADAPTATIONS ============
⚙

Adapt the walkthrough to your world

Patrícia's journey was meeting minutes. But thestandardSuitable for any long text that needs to become a structured deliverable:

📚 Recorded class → study sheet

Same structure: exact output structure (flashcards + map + 5 questions) + anti-hallucination rule ("don't invent concepts that weren't in class").

📞 Sales call → CRM report

"Client said X, Y, Z; objections A, B; next step W". Table. Each required column. No "several points were discussed."

📄 Long contract → risk summary

"List all clauses that: (a) imply a fine, (b) limit my termination, (c) have a term <30 days. Cite clause and page. If none exist, write 'none found'."

🎙 Podcast/interview → article

"Lead + 3 selected quotes + table of verifiable facts + 1 question that remained in the air". Journalistic structure.
