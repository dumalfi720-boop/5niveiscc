CASE 1

📧 Respond to thorny customer complaints

Scenario

Maria works in customer service at an online furniture store. Customer bought a wardrobe 40 days ago, delivery time was 30. Customer sent an email at 11pm yesterday:

"You sold me a phantom product. 40 days and nothing. I already bought another somewhere else. I want my money back NOW or I'm going to complain here, Procon, and sue."

Actual restrictions:the store cannot cancel the order (it has already left the factory, arriving in 5 days). Company policy: offers 15% discount + free shipping on future purchases. Maria has 30 minutes before the next call and it is her first day answering VIP.

❌ First try (which does NOT work)

Help me respond to this customer who complained about the delivery: "You sold me a phantom product..."

What Claude returned:a generic email "I'm sorry that happened, I understand your frustration, I apologize on behalf of the company" — without a concrete offer, without useful information, without the tone that customers expect from a caring brand. The client would respond “lero-lero” and things would escalate.

Why it failed:4 common N1 problems:

  • • Didn't saywho Claude must be(paper) — Claude became ChatGPT corporate standard
  • • Did not passrestriction context(cannot cancel, possible offer) — Claude invents what he cannot
  • • Didn't askformat(formal email? WhatsApp? phone?) — generic output that works for everything and nothing
  • • Not definedtone— the "empathetic robot" tone came out, the worst of both worlds

✅ Ultimate prompt that works

Act as a senior service manager for an online premium furniture brand. Tone: firm, human, without cliché “I’m sorry about what happened”. You admit the real mistake (10 days delay was beyond acceptable) without apologizing 3 times in the same sentence. Context: - Customer Maria Silva bought a wardrobe 40 days ago, deadline was 30 - The product left the factory yesterday, arriving in 5 working days (tracking: BR123456) - I cannot cancel the order at this stage - I can offer: 15% discount + free shipping on future purchases (valid 90 days) - Client threatened Reclame Aqui, Procon, lawsuit. You are rightly frustrated. Customer ordered: "You sold me a phantom product. 40 days and nothing. I already bought another somewhere else. I want my money back NOW or I'm going to complain here, Procon, and sue." Write the response email. Mandatory structure: 1. Direct acknowledgment of the error (1 short paragraph, without 'I apologize on behalf of the company') 2. New concrete information (arrival date, tracking) 3. Honest limit (I don't cancel at this stage + clear technical reason) 4. Concrete offer (discount + shipping) 5. Proactive next step (will I call you tomorrow at 2pm to confirm receipt?) Restrictions: - Max 180 words - Do not use "unfortunately", "we regret", "we understand your frustration" - Treat clients as adults, not as angry children - Personal ending: signature "Maria Silva — Customer Service [Store]"

📤 Expected output (real example)

Subject:Your wardrobe — delay confirmed and what I'm going to do

Mrs. Maria,

You're right to be irritated. Thirty days was the deadline, today it is forty, and no justification I give can erase that.

What I can give you now is concrete information: the product left the factory yesterday (tracking BR123456) and will arrive at your address between May 17th and 19th. At this stage I cannot cancel the shipment — the item is already on route.

Due to the delay, I will leave a 15% voucher + free shipping for any purchase in the next 90 days in your account. You only use it if you want.

I want to call you tomorrow at 2pm to confirm that everything is ok. If you prefer another time or another channel, reply to me here and I will adapt.

Maria Silva
Service [Store]

This is the kind of answer thatdoes not scalethe situation. Client feels they were treated like an adult, received new information, and have a clear next step.

🔀 4 variations we tested

Variation A — B2B Customer (corporate purchase):we changed "Ms. Maria" to name + position, removed voucher (B2B uses NC/discount on the next invoice), added "we will update PO and invoice" section.
Variation B — Customer is already a repeat buyer:we open by recognizing history ("you have been a customer since 2021"), we exchange voucher for something of greater value (express delivery next purchase, free assembly).
Variation C — Response via WhatsApp:same logic, but in 3-4 short messages, without subject, without formal signature, more conversational.
Variation D — If the process has already been opened:We replaced “I will not cancel at this stage” with more legally careful language, we added the line “I am sharing your case with our legal department for internal record purposes only”.

⚠ Common mistakes when adapting to your context

  • • Omit the actual deal restriction(what YOU can offer) — Claude invents policies or promises what you don't have.
  • • Forget "don't use these words"— "we're sorry", "unfortunately" come back immediately. Explicitly veto each one.
  • • Not taking the next concrete step(date, time, action) — without this, the customer is left with “ok, what now?” and things drag on.
  • • Treating customers like children("we understand your frustration") — adult recognizes adult. Direct tone is more respectful.

CASE 2

💡 Prepare “new ideas” meeting tomorrow 9am

Scenario

Carlos is a product manager at a B2B fintech (commission management for sales teams). Director sent yesterday at 6pm:"Tomorrow 9am, bring 5 ideas to increase retention of paying customers in the range of R$500-2000/month. Shows estimated ROI."

Actual restrictions:Carlos has 1h available tonight (children sleep 22h). He's tired, creativity on the floor. You have to submit 5 ideasdifferenteach other (not 5 variations of the same) and each with basic quantitative defense.

❌ First attempt

Give me 5 ideas to increase B2B customer retention

What Claude returned:"1. Loyalty program 2. Recurring NPS 3. Dedicated Customer Success 4. Better Onboarding 5. Quarterly meetings". Generic, unscaled, indistinguishable from any LinkedIn slide.

Why it failed:everything was missing. No product context (fintech B2B commission), no segment (R$500-2k/month), no difference criteria ("ideasdifferent", not variations), with no estimated ROI requirement.

✅ Ultimate prompt that works

Act as Director of Product Growth for a B2B SaaS fintech. You are skeptical of obvious "best practices" and prefer specific ideas with defensible ROI. Product context: - Fintech B2B that automates calculation and payment of commission for sales teams - ICP: companies with 5-50 salespeople - Current churn: 6% monthly on the R$500/month plan, 3% monthly on the R$2000/month plan - Top causes of churn (from interviews): (1) "I don't use it properly", (2) "I found a cheaper competitor", (3) "We changed CRM" - Average CAC: R$1200. Current LTV: R$8400. Task: Generate 5 ideas to reduce churn in this range. Each idea needs to be: - Specific (not "improve UX"; something you can start in 2 weeks) - DIFFERENT from the other 4 (distinct categories: product, commercial, customer success, integration, pricing — you can choose 5 of the 5) - With estimated impact on churn (in pp, with explicit assumption) - With estimated cost (R$ + people/week) - With major risk (what could go wrong) Format: markdown table with columns: Idea | Category | Why churn attacks | Estimated impact (pp) | Cost (R$/week) | Main risk At the end: ranking your 1st choice + 1 paragraph defending why. Restrictions: - Do not use "loyalty program", "NPS", "Customer Success" as an idea (they are clichés) - Each idea must have a specific premise number

📤 Excerpt from the output (1 of 5 ideas)

Idea 3 — "Native connector with the 3 most used CRMs"

Category:Integration

Why it attacks:38% of churn in the R$500 range cites “we changed CRM” as the cause. If the integration is native (and not via Zapier, which breaks), changing CRM is no longer a reason to change fintech.

Estimated impact:-1.5pp in monthly churn (premise: 50% of the 38% who cite CRM would stay if integration was plug-and-play; conservative).

Cost:R$60k (2 senior devs × 6 weeks). Recurring cost: ~R$5k/month of maintenance.

Main risk:CRMs change API in breakable ways. Mitigate with automated regression testing and vendor SLA.

Carlos arrives at the meeting with 5 of these. It's not "generic ideas" — it's a defensible proposal.

🔀 Tested variations

A — When you DO NOT have churn data because of:add "first suggest 3 ways to discover this information in 1 week, then generate ideas".
B — When the boss prefers visual presentation:exchange "markdown table" for "2x2 impact vs effort matrix, with each idea numbered".
C — For early-stage startups with little data:remove "quantitative premise", replace with "which 1-sprint experiment would validate this idea".
D — Without a B2B customer, it is B2C:context changes (NPS matters more, smaller ticket, higher volume). Prompt structure is the same.

⚠ Errors when adapting

  • • Forget the "veto of clichés"— "Loyalty program", "NPS", "Dedicated CSM" come back again and again. Explicitly veto.
  • • Asking for "different ideas" without categorizing— without explicit categories, Claude delivers 5 variations of the same family.
  • • Do not require numerical premises— without that it becomes a kick. "Explicit premise" forces reasoning.
  • • Just ask for ideas, without ranking— without ranking you arrive at the meeting with 5 alternatives and no defense. Boss waits for choice.

CASE 3

📚 Study dense chapter for test/competition

Scenario

Ana studies for the state inspector exam. You have 30 days until the test. There are 7 chapters left in the Tax Law book to review. Each chapter is ~50 pages. Ana no longer has 50 pages × 7 chapters × 2 time readings.

Actual restrictions:Ana needsmemorize, not just understand. The panel demands the letter of law (specific articles), distinctions between similar concepts (decadence vs prescription, fee vs public price), and gotchas in "except", "save", "only".

❌ First attempt

This Tax Law chapter sums it up for me: [pastes 50 pages]

What Claude returned:a nice 1-page summary explaining key concepts. Good to understand.Terrible to pass. It doesn't cover articles, it doesn't force memorization, it doesn't create pranks.

Why it failed:Ana requested the wrong output. "Summary" is the right output forto understand. Formemorize and answer banking, the right output is "flashcards + unpublished questions + comparative table + list of gotchas".

✅ Final prompt (active study)

Act as a senior Tax Law professor specializing in public tenders. Its role is to generate ACTIVE (not passive) review material. I will paste a chapter from the book below. For this chapter, generate 4 different outputs, in order: 1. **Visual map in text** (hierarchical structure): root concept → branches → exceptions. Use indentation. Max 30 lines. Must fit on 1 screen. 2. **15 flashcards** in the format: - Front: direct question or partial definition ("tax decay occurs when...") - Back: response with cited article of law (e.g.: "Art. 173, CTN — in 5 years counted...") Mix: 5 on definitions, 5 on specific articles, 5 on exceptions. 3. **10 new questions in the FCC panel style** (alternatives A-E, 1 correct). Include at least: - 3 questions with "except/saved/only" (classic pranks) - 2 questions asking for distinction between similar concepts - 2 questions asking deadlines/dates/numeric limits - 3 mixed questions At the end, a line-by-line commented answer for each alternative explaining why it is right or wrong. 4. **Trap table**: each line is a common confusion on the topic (e.g.: "decay X prescription"), with 3 columns: What looks the same | The critical difference | How does the bank ask this? Restrictions: - ALWAYS cite articles of law when applicable (CTN, CF, specific laws) - Don't invent jurisprudence. If you're not sure, say so. - Do not use academic jargon. Those who are studying need clarity, not erudition. Chapter: [paste here]

📤 Excerpt from the output (1 of 15 flashcards + 1 of 10 questions)

Flashcard 7/15

Front:When does the main tax obligation arise?

Verse:With the occurrence of the triggering event (Art. 113, §1º, CTN). Distinguish from "ancillary obligation" that arises from tax legislation (§2) — not from the triggering event itself.

Question 4 / 10 (FCC-style)

The reciprocal tax immunity provided for in art. 150, VI, "a" of CF/88 does NOT apply:

A) to the assets of the Union, States and Municipalities among themselves

B) to autarchies and foundations established by the Public Power in relation to assets linked to essential purposes

C) to assets related to the exploration of economic activity governed by rules of private law

D) to temples of any cult

E) active debt arising from taxes

Answer key: C.Comment: §3 of art. 150 expressly removes immunity when the public entity explores economic activity in a private regime (competition) — the bank loves to charge this §3. The others: A is the caput, B is §2 (included), D is item "b" (religious immunity, another item), E does not exist in the CF — gotcha.

🔀 Tested variations

A — CESPE/Cebraspe banking competition (Right/Wrong):change the format of the 10 questions to "Right or Wrong" + comment. CESPE takes a lot of subtleties — adjusting “gotchas” for this format.
B — For review the day before (10 min):just the visual map + trap table (no flashcards/questions). It's quick review, not learning.
C — For first reading (understand, not memorize):just the visual map + "explain each node as if I had never studied it" — skip questions.
D — Subjects other than Law:Medicine, Engineering, Banking Exams — same structure, replace "Art. CTN" with "specific source of the subject".

⚠ Errors when adapting

  • • Request "summary" without specifying use— understand ≠ memorize ≠ answer the test. Each objective requires different output.
  • • Do not veto invention of jurisprudence— Claude invents STF/STJ with a name that sounds real. Always say "if you're not sure, say so; don't make it up."
  • • Forgetting to ask for commented feedback— question without comment you memorize answer without understanding error.
  • • Do not specify bankroll— FCC, CESPE, FGV, IBFC have different "personalities". Each one snakes in a specific way.

============ ABSTRACTED PATTERN ============
★

🧠 The pattern behind the 3 cases

Did you notice that the final 3 prompts follow the same anatomy? It's no coincidence. It's the structure that Claude needs to deliver real value in N1, in any area:

  1. 1. Specific role— don’t “act as an assistant”, but “act as a director of B2B fintech Product Growth”. The niche informs the tone, the vocabulary, what it refuses.
  2. 2. Context with real restrictions— what you CAN and CANNOT offer. Without that, Claude invents.
  3. 3. Task decomposed into mandatory structure— "1. do X. 2. do Y. 3. do Z." Numbered list > "make a good email".
  4. 4. Explicit vetoes— "don't use these words", "don't invent jurisprudence", "don't use these cliché ideas".
  5. 5. Next step/output format— exactly how Claude should deliver. Table? E-mail? Bullets? How many items?

Memorize these 5 and you will leave N1 with 60% of the value unlocked. You memorize the case prompts without understanding this structure, and when the context changes you reproduce bad prompts.
