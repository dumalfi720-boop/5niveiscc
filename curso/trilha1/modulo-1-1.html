MODULE 1.1 · ~25 min

💬 Mastering the basics that no one masters

Most stay at Level 1 because they treat Claude like Google. Here you learn how to get 3x more value from the same chat — no upgrade, no new tool, no complications.

SECTION 1
1

🎯 What characterizes Level 1

You open Claude, ask a question, copy the answer and close it. Maybe ask them to write an email, explain a concept, generate a short script. Nothing more. Chat without memory, without project, without integration.

This is the "like search that returns paragraphs" usage. It's useful — saves ~30 min/day on small tasks. But it's the floor. And 90% of people stay here because they believe that this is enough.

📊 How to identify that you are on N1:

  • • You use Claude on the web, without having configured Projects
  • • Every conversation starts from scratch — you paste the same context every time
  • • Did not connect any tools (Slack, Drive, Gmail)
  • • You rarely exceed 5 messages in a conversation
SECTION 2
2

✍ Anatomy of a prompt that delivers

Most of the bad answers aren't Claude's fault — it's vague prompting. Always use this 4-part framework:

  1. 1. Role:"Act like a senior B2B SaaS copywriter."
  2. 2. Context:"I'm creating a welcome email for customers who have just signed up for a R$990/month plan."
  3. 3. Format:"Give me 3 different versions, in bullets, with subject + body."
  4. 4. Restriction:"Maximum 80 words each. Tone: confident, no marketing cliché."

💡 Practical tip:If your first answer was generic, don't give up — add restriction. "Now more direct" / "Without using words X, Y, Z" / "Instead, think like [example]." Iterating is free.

SECTION 3
3

📸 Screenshots: the most underutilized skill

Claude reads images. You can paste screenshots of spreadsheets, emails, error screens, photos of tables and graphs — and ask for an explanation, transcription, improvement or analysis. Half of the people on N1 are typing what a print would solve in 2 seconds.

✅ Cases where print > text

  • • Software error ("what does this stack mean?")
  • • Excel table ("translates to markdown")
  • • Configuration screen ("explains each field")
  • • Meeting board (“transcribe and organize”)
  • • Graph ("what does this data suggest?")

⌨ Shortcuts to capture quickly

  • • Mac: Cmd+Shift+4 (area) / Ctrl+Cmd+Shift+4 (clipboard)
  • • Win: Win+Shift+S (Snip & Sketch)
  • • Linux: Print Screen / Flameshot
  • • Mobile: prints + paste directly into the Claude app
SECTION 4
4

📝 The 6 practical uses for everyday life

They cover 80% of what you will order in N1. Memorize all 6 — it will get rid of the "blank page."

1. Write better

Emails, messages, posts, descriptions. Paste the draft and ask "rewrite more [adjective]" / "shorten it to X words" / "change the tone to Y".

2. Summarize content

Articles, transcripts, reports. "Summarize in 5 bullets, with 1 practical action at the end." Paste the text in sequence.

3. Explain difficult subjects

"Explain [concept] as if I were [persona]." Personas that work: 10 year old, manager without a technician, first day of internship.

4. Create ideas

"Give me 10 different ideas for [problem]. Then group them into 3 categories and tell me which one I should start with first." Volume + curation.

5. Correct and revise

"Point out errors in grammar, clarity, and tone in this text. Show corrections as diff." Useful for contracts, proposals, formal emails.

6. Create small scripts or formulas

"Excel formula for adding cells where column A is X." / "Python script to rename files." Paste the result, test, adjust.

SECTION 5
5

⚠ The 4 mistakes that keep you at Level 1

❌ Treat like Google

Ask with 3 words and expect a perfect answer. Claude is not a seeker — he is a collaborator.

❌ Do not iterate

Accept the 1st answer without refining. The 2nd question is worth more than the 1st — almost always.

❌ Vague prompt

"Help me with this" says nothing. Always: role + context + format + restriction.

❌ Do not review

Claude makes mistakes with confidence. Review numbers, quotations, and proper nouns before using.

SECTION 7
7

🚪 When Level 1 is enough

Leveling up unnecessarily becomes wasted effort. But staying when work already demands continuity means leaving 5 hours/week on the table. Use these criteria:

✅ Stay at N1 if…

  • • You use it to answer specific questions
  • • Each order is different from the previous one
  • • There is no material that can be used repeatedly
  • • Works alone, without recurring clients

⬆ Go up to N2 if…

  • • You paste the same context every time
  • • Repeats the same type of order throughout the week
  • • Do you have docs/PDFs that you always reference
  • • Serves recurring clients/projects
