MODULE 1.1
1.1~25 min

💬 Mastering the basics that no one masters

How to get 3x more value from pure chat before moving on to the next level.

What is it:

The level where Claude is used as a quest: you open it, ask it, copy the answer and close it. No memory, no project, no integration.

Why learn:

It's anyone's floor. Recognizing that you are here is the prerequisite for rising. 90% of people stay with it because they think it's "enough."

Key concepts:

Stateless chat · Single reply · ~30 min/day saved · Low ceiling but safe.

What is it:

Simple structure for any order: define thepaper("act as senior copywriter"), givecontext("it's an email for B2B customers"), ask for theformat("3 versions in bullets") and putrestriction("max. 80 words each").

Why learn:

Most of the bad answers aren't Claude's fault — it's vague prompting. This pattern doubles the quality without changing tools.

Key concepts:

Clear role · Sufficient context · Explicit format · Measurable constraint · Iterate with “now do more X”.

What is it:

Claude reads images. You can paste screenshots of spreadsheets, emails, error screens, photos of tables and graphs — and ask for an explanation, transcription, improvement or analysis.

Why learn:

Half of the work in Level 1 is describing in text what a print would solve in 2 seconds. Whoever cheats, saves an absurd amount of time.

Key concepts:

Ctrl+Shift+S on Mac/Win · Table transcription · Graph analysis · Error reading · Comparison of two screens.

What is it:

Six standards that cover 80% of Level 1 usage: improve texts, summarize long content, explain difficult concepts, generate ideas, review errors, create short formulas/scripts.

Why learn:

You don't need to "know it all" — you need to recognize which of the 6 patterns fits what you're doing now.

Key concepts:

"Rewrite more formal" · "Summarize in 5 bullets" · "Explain to a child" · "10 different ideas" · "Point out errors" · "Excel Formula for X".

What is it:

Asking for 1-word answers as if you were a search engine · accepting the 1st answer without refining · generic prompts ("help me with this") · not reviewing factual or tone errors.

Why learn:

These 4 habits are the invisible ceiling of Level 1. Eliminating them already gives you half the jump to Level 2.

Key concepts:

Iterating is free · Revising is mandatory · Claude makes mistakes with confidence · 2nd question is worth more than the 1st.

What is it:

Collection of copy-and-paste prompts for each of the 6 uses. Not to decorate — to adapt.

Why learn:

Taking the "blank page" out of the equation speeds up usage. After 1 week you can create your own.

Key concepts:

Parameterizable templates · Variables between {keys} · Save the best ones · Share with the team.

What is it:

Honest criteria: if you use Claude for occasional and disposable things, Level 1 is enough. If you find yourself repeating the same type of task every week, it's time to step up.

Why learn:

Leveling up unnecessarily becomes wasted effort. But staying when work already demands continuity means leaving 5 hours/week on the table.

Key concepts:

"Are you copying the same answer?" → climb · "Do you rewrite the context every time?" → climb · "Do you just use it to clear up doubts?" → stay.

What is it:

For 7 days, record every real task you solved with Claude in the chat. Write down prompt + quality of response + how many iterations.

Why learn:

Without data, you think you use it well. With dice, you discover your patterns and bottlenecks.

Key concepts:

Simple spreadsheet · 3 columns (task, prompt, iterations) · Review on Sunday · Identify repeated pattern = Project candidate (N2).

MODULE 1.2
1.2~20 min

🚀 Cheat Code: your first Project

The unique move that changes you from Level 1 to Level 2 without becoming a technician.

What is it:

Project is a space with fixed instruction + knowledge base. Every conversation is born preloaded with its context.

Why learn:

It's the free feature that most separates those who save 30 minutes/day from those who save 5 hours/week.

Key concepts:

Fixed instruction · Knowledge base · 1 theme per project · "Starts loaded".

What is it:

Good topic for the first project: something you play every week, has reference material (PDF, docs, examples), and speaks to a defined audience.

Why learn:

Generic project = low ROI. Specific project = you feel the difference in the first conversation.

Key concepts:

"Customer service at store X" > "General marketing" · "Content for company Y's Instagram" > "Social media".

What is it:

3 layers: (1) system prompt — who you are, how Claude should respond, (2) knowledge base — reference PDFs/docs, (3) history — previous conversations.

Why learn:

Knowing the 3 layers avoids the most common mistake: stacking everything in the prompt and nothing in the docs.

Key concepts:

Prompt < 1 page · Docs in PDF/MD · 1 conversation, 1 scope · Active memory.

What is it:

Ready template: "You are my [role]. You work with [context]. Whenever I ask for X, do Y. Never do Z. Answer in [format]."

Why learn:

Without a template, everyone writes a generic prompt. With template, in 5 minutes you have usable instructions.

Key concepts:

Light paper · Always/Never · Fixed format · Tone of voice · Language.

What is it:

You created a Project, used it for 1 week, realized that you still copy things from Drive/Slack manually. This discomfort is the trigger for N2.

Why learn:

Going up for the right inconvenience, not for fashion. N2 is only valid if you feel the friction of continuing to manualize.

Key concepts:

"I wish he could get this file on his own" · "This Excel would deserve a deck" · Practical signs > theoretical signs.

What is it:

7 items: chosen topic · written instructions · attached docs · 1 pilot conversation run · you reviewed errors · adjusted the prompt · feel the friction of manualizing.

Why learn:

Jumping without a checklist = chaos on N2. With a checklist, you reach the next level with a solid foundation.

Key concepts:

Everything on paper · Pilot conversation · Identified friction · Next step: connectors.

MODULE 1.3 - COMPLETE CASES
1.3~40 min · COMPLETE CASES

🎯 Real cases worked-through

Three real situations, each with a failed attempt, an exact fit, and a final prompt that delivers. Standard 5 ingredients distilled to the end.

📧 Case 1 — Responding to a thorny customer complaint

Maria, customer service, customer threatens Procon. Restriction: Cannot cancel, 15% offer available. See the wrong prompt, why, the final prompt that works, 4 variations and 4 common mistakes when adapting.

💡 Case 2 — 5 ideas with estimated ROI for tomorrow 9am

Carlos, fintech B2B product manager. Director asked for 5 different ideas to retain customers R$500-2k/month. See how he went from "generic ideas" to a defensible proposal with a numerical premise and ranking.

📚 Case 3 — Active study for the competition (not passive)

Ana, 30 days for the tax exam. 7 dense chapters. See how "summary" is the wrong output for memorizing — flashcards + 10 exam-style questions + trap table is the right output.

🧠 Bonus — the 5 ingredient standard

The 3 cases follow the same anatomy. The 5 ingredients that unlock any N1 prompt, distilled for you to adapt to any area.

MODULE 1.4 - WALKTHROUGH
1.4~45 min · CINEMATOGRAPHIC WALKTHROUGH

🎬 From the 78-minute meeting to the minutes that everyone reads

A complete day, without cuts, from Wednesday 3:35 pm (CEO sent WhatsApp) until Thursday 9:12 am (Council approved). 3 iterations, 2 different outputs from the same source, 0 hallucinations.

Iteration 0 — the bad reflection:"give me a summary." Generic output, no use.
Iteration 1 — first adjustment:now with questions from the CEO. Better, but Claude hallucinated names.
Iteration 2 — tightening context and veto:exact structure + anti-vagueness rules + permission to say "I don't know". It works.
Iteration 3 — CEO asks for another cut:"Council slide version, 6 lines". 30 seconds, same conversation.
📊 Real metrics: 63 min saved, 2 different outputs from the same source, 0 hallucinations in the end. All in pure chat.
