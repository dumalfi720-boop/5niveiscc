MODULE 5.5 · WALKTHROUGH · ~50 min

🎬 From the idea "I'm going to sell PR review as a service" to the first customer R$ 12k/month in 21 days

Follow João, a former tech lead who recently left a scale-up. You see it day by day: the open-source prototype that opens doors, the cold outbound that works, the decisive demo, the proposal with transparent pricing, and the first 21 days with a paying customer.

⚠ This is the most "commercial" module of the course.Here the focus is on monetizing N5 — you need the technical content from the previous modules to do the work, but what separates "building" from "selling" is the sequence of the next 3 weeks.

SETUP
⚙

Setup — João, day 0

Who:João, 38, 12 years as a dev, last 3 as a tech lead in a scale-up that he sold to an American competitor. It came out with an 8-month shelf life. You want to start a business but don't want to "create another agency".

The idea:"Small companies (10-30 devs) rarely have CTO/staff who do consistent code review. I can deliver this as a service using Claude Code routines + my experience."

Premise he will test:B2B startups of 10-30 devs with Series A would pay R$8-15k/month for "24/7 code review with weekly human curation" — cheap substitute for hiring staff engineers.

The rule he imposes:ZERO development without confirmed customer. Validate before building.

DAY 1-3
1

Days 1-3 — public prototype + tuned ICP

Day 1 (Monday) — Surgical PCI

João opens Claude and asks for help at the ICP:

John:"I want to validate 'automated code review as a service'. It helps me design surgical ICP — not 'B2B startups', but something VERY specific. Objective criteria + list of 30 companies that fit (LinkedIn + Crunchbase)."

Claude (with web search):Tuned ICP: Brazilian B2B SaaS, Series A or profitable bootstrapped, 10-30 devs (sweet spot 15-22), no senior CTO (signal: CTO < 30 years OR no CTO position), modern tech stack (Node/Python/Go), public GitHub OR website shows repo. List of 30 candidates generated with Linkedin link and name of the head of eng/CTO.

João reviews, removes 4 outside the ICP, adds 6 that he knows personally. Final list: 32 accounts. Time spent: 3 hours.

Day 2 (Tuesday) — open-source prototype

João’s strategic decision:publish basic open-source version on GitHub.Reason: proves that he knows how to do it, generates trust, low friction for the demo. The paid version will be "tuned + weekly human curation + multi-repo + custom rules".

João assembles repoprr-reviewer(Pull Request Reviewer) in 6 hours:

  • • GitHub Action that triggers on pull_request opened/synchronize
  • • Calls Claude Code via headless SDK (claude -p) with review prompt
  • • Post inline comments + final summary as "comment", not "approve"
  • • Cinematic README with GIF showing it working + "how to install in 5 min"
  • • MIT license

Publish on GitHub, make 1 post on LinkedIn explaining.

Day 3 (Wednesday) — first organic reaction

Post has 14k views, 240 likes, 38 comments. 3 developers install it in their own repo, send a printout of the first reviews. Joana, head of eng at one of João’s 32 target companies, comments on the post:

"Man, this is cool. I'd better test it in production. DM me."

João has the first inbound lead before the outbound even starts.

DAY 4-7
2

Days 4-7 — surgical cold outbound

The message that John sends

João wrote a prompt in Claude with the rule: "10 different messages for the 32 ICP, each one personalized with something from their GitHub that proves that I looked." The emerging default message:

"Hi [Name], I saw that you guys are growing well [specific insight from the site/Linkedin/repo]. I noticed on your Github that [concrete technical observation — e.g. 'a lot of PR going 12 hours+ without review' OR 'there are 4 senior devs who became reviewers'].

I just published an open-source routine by Claude Code that does automatic code review:github.com/joao/prr-reviewer. 240 likes on Linkedin today, 3 teams already testing.

I'm setting up a paid version (weekly human curation + multi-repo + company custom rules) and I'm looking for 2 pilot clients. R$5k/month for the pilot (final version R$12k). Would you like a 15-minute demo to see if it makes sense?"

Result in 4 days:32 submissions, 11 responses, 4 marked demos. Absurd response rate (34%) from triple punch:open-source visible + personalized technical insight + cheap pilot offer.

DAY 8 - DEMO
3

Day 8 (Tuesday) — decisive demo with Joana

João comes prepared: before the call, he created a fork of 3 public repos similar to Joana's company stack, simulated realistic PRs, ran the routine, captured screenshots. In the call:

  • • First 3 min:Doesn't sell. Ask “what’s your PR review flow like today?” Joana describes: PRs spend 14 hours on average waiting, 2 senior devs do 70% of the reviews, they complain.
  • • Min 3-8:Share screen. Shows a real PR on the fork. Demonstrates: webhook fires, routine runs in 4 minutes, inline comments appear. Joana visibly engages.
  • • Min 8-12:Shows dashboard he built to show "how much review burden I would take off your team". Projected metrics: -65% average time to 1st review, -70% load on 2 senior devs.
  • • Min 12-13:Question: "How much do you invest in code quality today, adding dev time + Linter + SonarCloud?" Joana responds: ~R$ 18k/month equivalent.
  • • Min 13-14:João: "My proposal: R$ 12k/month in the paid version. Includes weekly human curation from me (I review what Claude reviewed, point out patterns), customization of rules for your context, multi-repo (up to 5), SLA of 15 minutes for the 1st review. 30-day pilot for R$ 5k."
  • • Min 14-15:Joana asks for 2 days to discuss with the CTO.Friday closes the pilot.
DAY 9-14
4

Days 9-14 — contract, setup, first operational week

Day 9-10 — contract and security

João had it ready since day 2 (he prepared it together with the prototype):

  • • Clear SLA: 95% PRs with 1st review in ≤15min, accessible kill switch, authority (does not work on PRs with a "human-only" label)
  • • NDA + data processing: GitHub App scoped (does not see code outside authorized repos), all via Anthropic API enterprise (no log retention), monthly log backup
  • • Exit clause: customer can cancel at any time within 7 days, without penalty
  • • "Jewell customer" model for the first 3: pilot R$5k, then R$12k. Customer can give public feedback (case study) in exchange for R$1k off for 6 months.

Signed on the 10th.

DAY 15-21

Days 15-21 — weekly curation + second client

Day 16 — weekly curation session (the “human” of the service)

João dedicates 2 hours every Tuesday to "auditing" what Claude reviewed this week. Look for patterns. This is what João sells in addition to automation — it’s what justifies R$12k instead of R$1,500.

Delivery to the client: weekly report on 2 pages:

  • • 1 systemic problem (not caught in individual PR) that is worth a conversation

Joana loves it. Forward to the CTO. CTO comments: "This report is worth the price alone."

LESSONS
★

Lessons from the journey

1. Open-source is the best business card

João opened doors with technical proof, not with pitch. Paid version sells AFTER the customer already trusts. Without open-source, his outbound would be a 5% response rate instead of 34%.

2. Surgical PCI > "all startups"

32 accounts chosen with strict criteria. Each message showed real knowledge of the context. Seller who sends 1,000 identical messages receives a 1% response rate. João received 34%.

3. Human curation is what differentiates a product from a service

Claude does 90% of the work automatically. João brings the remaining 10% in the weekly report — this 10% is what justifies R$12k vs R$1,500. "See-the-trend-report" became the CTO's habit.

4. Transparent pricing disarms negotiation

João said exactly what it costs (R$ 12k full, R$ 5k pilot, R$ 1k off in case exchange). The customer understood there was no need to haggle — the price was reasonable and clear.

5. SLA + exit clause > lock-in

"Cancel in 7 days without fine" greatly lowers the entry barrier. Those who deliver real value do not need lock-in to retain customers.
