🚀 Cheat Code: stop copying and pasting

The mental habit that makes N2 yield 5 hours/week — and prepares you to jump to N3 without suddenly becoming a technician.

1

📅 Map your week: where you still manualize

For 5 days, write down every time you copy-pasted between tools (Slack→Claude, Drive→Claude, Claude→email, etc.). Use 3 columns:Source → Destination → Frequency.

SourceDestinationFrequencyTime/use
Slack #salesClaude to summarize3x/day~5 min
Drive — proposalsClaude to adapt5x/week~10 min
Gmail — clientsClaude to respond10x/day~3 min

On Sunday, order by "total time". The first 3 become connectors. The rest is in pure chat.

2

🔁 Recipe: 1 project + 1-2 connectors + 1 standard prompt

The N2 "real productivity" standard has 3 parts:

  1. 1.One project per weekly ritual (not per generic area).
  2. 2.1-2 connectors that point to the source of the ritual (not all 50).
  3. 3.A standard prompt saved in the project that you re-run without rewriting ("I want my summary for the week with X, Y, Z").

Result: you open the project on Friday, tell it to "run", download the file, send it. Without typing context.

3

📚 3 ready-made case studies

Case 1 — Content creation

Project:"Instagram content from [brand X]"
Connectors:Google Drive (brand book + historical post), Notion (editorial calendar)
Standard prompt:"Generate 5 posts for the next week, following the brand's tone, alternating between tip, testimonial and CTA. Output in table: day, format, copy."

Case 2 — Customer service

Project:"Z store support"
Connectors:Gmail, Slack #support
Standard prompt:"Sorting this morning's tickets: urgency, category, suggested response. Use the project's FAQ base. Mark cases that require a human."

Case 3 — Studies

Project:"Contest X — current notice"
Connectors:Drive (announcement, templates), Calendar (schedule)
Standard prompt:"Generates 20 new questions on [theme of the week], in panel style. Include commented answer sheet citing the article of the law."

4

⚠ The 5 mistakes that stop you in N2

❌ Too generic design

"Marketing" does not become a good project. "IG content brand X" yes.

❌ No docs attached

Empty project is just pure chat with baggage.

❌ No fixed instruction

Rewrite context entire conversation = back to N1.

❌ Mix 3 subjects

1 project = 1 scope. Create another one for the next one.

❌ Do not review results

Memory captures errors together. Review outputs and adjust instruction when you notice drift.

5

🚨 Ceiling sign: "I wish he would do it on my computer"

There comes a time when you want Claude to do something on your system: organize a folder, edit local files, run a long sequence without you copying/pasting. This is the ceiling of N2 — N3 (co-work) begins.

Recognizing the signal is what avoids "squeezing" the N2 and delaying the jump. When you find yourself thinking "if only it could open this file here", it's time.

6

🧭 Preparing the ground for co-work (Level 3)

Before you even enable co-work, do the manual pre-work of structuring your workbook. This turns an "unpredictable Claude" into a "coworker Claude":

~/work/
├── 00_about_me/ ← who you are, how you work
├── 01_templates/ ← protected templates (never edit)
├── 02_projects/ ← one per client/initiative
├── 03_entries/ ← raw files to process
├── 04_processing/ ← work in progress
├── 05_outputs/ ← final deliveries (versioned)
└── 06_file/ ← history closed

When enabling co-work on N3, simply say "read 00_about_me first. Never edit 01_templates. Always deliver in 05_outputs." Claude becomes a coworker because he has ground rules.
