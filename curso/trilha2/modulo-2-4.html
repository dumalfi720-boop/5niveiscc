MODULE 2.4 · WALKTHROUGH · ~50 min

🎬 Of the 247 messy responses to the Artifact that became the team's ritual

Pedro, head of product at a B2B edtech, leaves a tense Friday with 247 NPS responses in free text and the CEO wanting "insights on Monday at 9am". You follow the 5 iterations until it delivers not a report, but an interactive tool that the entire team starts using.

⚠ Attention:This walkthrough shows a movement that separates N2 from N1:stop generating static reports and start delivering living artifacts. Whoever masters this delivers 5x the value with the same session.

SETUP
⚙

Setup — Friday, 5:22 pm

Who:Pedro, 36, head of product at a B2B edtech that sells school management systems (250 customers, average ticket R$2.4k/month). It's already on N2 — it has the "Edtech NPS" Project configured, with a Drive connector.

What happened:Quarterly NPS closed yesterday. 247 responses, 189 of which were free text. Promoters 31%, Detractors 24%, Neutrals 45% — NPS = 7. Dropped 6 points vs. previous quarter.

Pressure:CEO sent Slack 4pm:

"Pedro, NPS dropped 6 points. Monday 9am has all-hands. I need all leadership to enter this meeting knowing: (a) what is hurting the product, (b) where it is concentrated, (c) what can be changed in 30 days. Don't send me a 40-slide report."

Stakes:Pedro has a whole weekend (and wants to enjoy it with his family). Every hour tonight is worth two today on Sunday. CEO asks for actionable insight, not pretty dashboard.

ITERATION 0
0

Attempt 0 — the anxious PM reflex (5:28 p.m.)

Pedro opens the Project, pastes the CSV (189 responses in free text), types:

PETER →
Analyze these NPS responses and give me insights for the CEO. Focus on what's bad.

Claude returns:

← CLAUDE
After analysis, I identified some main themes in the responses: 1. **Performance:** many users mentioned slowness 2. **Support:** some customers expressed dissatisfaction with the service 3. **Features:** there are requests for new features 4. **Onboarding:** new customers reported initial difficulties I recommend investigating these areas in more depth.

😬 Pedro takes a deep breath

This is exactly the kind of "insight" the CEO hates. "Many", "some", "there are requests" — total vagueness. No number. No customer. With nothing actionable.

Why it failed (and why EVERY average N2 stops here):Pedro asked for "analysis" without defining CATEGORIES, without COUNTING, without real QUOTE, without SEGMENTATION. Claude defaulted to a generic executive summary — exactly what the CEO asked NOT to send.

Pedro thinks, "I need to force Claude to be specific." Rewrite:

Identify sentiment (Promoter, Neutral, Detractor) — it’s already in column B 3. Select the segment (it's already in column C: small/medium/large school) 4. Extract 1 short quote (15 words max) per answer Output: markdown table with the 6 categories, count by sentiment, count by segment, and 3 representative quotes per category.

✅ Aha moment

Pedro reads and realizes thatClaude is now an analysis partner, not a pattern seeker. The 3 changes that unlocked:

LESSONS
★

Lessons from the journey

1. “Analysis” without categories = vagueness

Iteration 0 failed because asking for "analysis" leaves Claude at the "many customers cited performance" level. Forcing explicit categories + counting + segmentation unlocks real quantitative reasoning.

2. Literal quote > paraphrase

The "Performance ≠ Boleto" error only appeared because Pedro asked for a literal quote. When Claude paraphrases, he interprets — and wrong interpretation becomes wrong category becomes wrong insight.

3. Permission to [?] = Claude becomes a partner

"If you're not sure, check [?] and list me" turned Claude from "I'm going to guess so I seem useful" to "I'm going to separate what's certain from what's ambiguous." 3 minutes of Pedro decided 8 cases. It's worth gold.

4. PDF is the wrong output when the team needs to drill

The N2 movement that separates Pedro from the majority was: changing "generates report" for "generates interactive artifact". PDF dies in the folder; tool becomes ritual. CEO talked about "this becomes a quarterly ritual" — he didn't ask, he realized.

5. Preconfigured Design made EVERYTHING faster

Pedro didn't start from scratch. There was already an "Edtech NPS" Project with instructions + Drive connector. That's why 2h30 covered the mess to the published artifact. Without the Project ready, this takes 5-6 hours.
